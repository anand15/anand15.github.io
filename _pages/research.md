My work sits at the intersection of primate ecology, political ecology and environmental history, and is grounded in India. Four threads run through it.

Primate behaviour in human-modified landscapes
======
How do macaques live in landscapes that people have reshaped? I work on ranging, foraging and collective movement in rhesus and bonnet macaques across agricultural, plantation and mining landscapes, using focal and scan sampling of habituated groups. Current work in southern Karnataka examines how bonnet macaques negotiate a plantation-agriculture mosaic, and what that negotiation costs them socially as well as energetically.

Conflict and coexistence as categories
======
"Human-wildlife conflict" is not a neutral description of what happens when people and animals share space. It is a framing with a history, and it does work — directing attention to some problems and away from others. Several of my papers examine how the category was produced, whether the escalation it assumes is empirically supported, and what gets missed when disputes between people are recorded as disputes between people and animals.

Commons, forest rights and land governance
======
Common lands are poorly recorded in Indian land data, and the gaps are not random. I work on what administrative and satellite data can and cannot tell us about the extent and condition of commons, and on the implementation of Community Forest Rights — currently in Kalahandi district, Odisha.

Spatial and quantitative methods for development research
======
Alongside the substantive work, I develop and teach spatial and quantitative methods: multi-scale analysis across district, village and point level; terrain-adjusted accessibility; land-use classification; and computational text analysis of ecological literature.

Field sites
======
* **Southern Karnataka** — bonnet macaque, plantation and agricultural mosaic (2024–2025)
* **Solan, Himachal Pradesh** — rhesus macaque, forest-agriculture mosaic (2016–2017)
* **Mancherial and Peddapalli, Telangana** — rhesus macaque, coal-mining landscape (2019)
* **Kalahandi, Odisha** — commons and forest rights
